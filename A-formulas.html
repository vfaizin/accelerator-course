Appendix A: constants, units and formulas

A formula sheet for the whole course: first the key formulas of Part 0, Foundations, then the physical constants, the unit conversions, the practical formulas used every day, and then, chapter by chapter, the results each chapter derives, in that chapter’s own notation, with a link to where the derivation is.

How to use this sheet

Nothing here is derived. Each formula is stated as its chapter states it, with its symbols and units, one line on what it means, and a link to the section that derives it or says where it is quoted from. Read the chapter before trusting the formula: every one of them rests on approximations that the chapter names, and most fail somewhere.

The course’s conventions hold throughout, and a few are easy to forget:

  • \(W\) is kinetic energy, \(E = \gamma mc^2\) total energy, \(T\) temperature.
  • The electric field is \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\), of size \(\mathcal{E}\) (V/m), in Part 0 and in every chapter, to keep it apart from an energy \(E\). Its components and amplitudes keep the script letter: \(\mathcal{E}_z\), \(\mathcal{E}_0\), \(\mathcal{E}_{acc}\).
  • The energy gain at a gap is \(\Delta W = q\hat{V}\sin\phi\), with \(\phi\) growing for later arrival: \(\phi = 90^\circ\) is the crest. Linear-accelerator texts write \(q\mathcal{E}_0TL\cos\phi\), with their \(T\) the course’s transit-time factor \(T_t\); then \(\phi_{\text{sine}} = \phi_{\text{cosine}} + 90^\circ\).
  • Shunt impedance is the linac definition \(R_{sh} = \hat{V}^2/P\). Circuit and synchrotron texts use \(\hat{V}^2/2P\), half of it; so does chapter 22 for its resonator impedances.
  • Twiss functions always carry a plane subscript, \(\beta_x\), \(\alpha_y\), \(\gamma_x\), to keep them apart from the relativistic \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\).
  • Luminosity is quoted in cm\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\).

Where a section heading contains mathematics, its link goes to the chapter and the text names the section number, because GitHub’s anchors for such headings are unreliable.

0. Part 0: the foundations

The formulas each Foundations chapter lists under “Equations to remember”, grouped by chapter, with their symbols and units and a link to the section that explains them. They need only high-school mathematics and the Foundations chapters before them, and every later chapter uses them without saying so. The chapters from 1 on derive the relativistic forms where Part 0 is non-relativistic: chapter 1 replaces \(\tfrac12mv^2\) and \(\mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}\) by \((\gamma - 1)mc^2\) and \(\gamma m\mathbf{v}\).

F1 Calculus

  • Derivative. \(\dfrac{df}{dx} = \lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\): the slope of \(y = f(x)\) at \(x\), in units of \(y\) per unit of \(x\). Section 1.
  • Rules. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^n = nx^{n-1}\); product \((fg)' = f'g + fg'\); chain \(\dfrac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))\,g'(x)\). Section 2.
  • Sine, cosine, exponential, logarithm. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x\), \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x\), with \(x\) in radians; \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x\); \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x = \dfrac1x\). Section 3, section 4.
  • Small changes. \(\Delta f \approx f'(x)\,\Delta x\); for \(f = x^n\), \(\Delta f/f \approx n\,\Delta x/x\). How errors grow, and when the rule fails. Section 5.
  • Fundamental theorem. \(\displaystyle\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx = f(b) - f(a)\): adding up the small changes gives the whole change. Section 7.
  • Taylor series. \(f(a + h) = f(a) + f'(a)\,h + \tfrac12f''(a)\,h^2 + \tfrac16f'''(a)\,h^3 + \dots\); in particular \((1 + x)^n \approx 1 + nx\) for small \(x\). Section 8.
  • Decay. \(dN/dt = -\lambda N\) gives \(N = N_0e^{-\lambda t}\), with rate \(\lambda\) (s\(^{-1}\)) and time constant \(\tau = 1/\lambda\) (s). Section 10.
  • Oscillation. \(d^2x/dt^2 = -\omega^2x\) gives \(x = A\cos\omega t + B\sin\omega t\), with \(\omega\) in rad/s. Section 10.

F2 Vectors and vector calculus

  • Components. \(\mathbf{A} = A_x\hat{\mathbf{x}} + A_y\hat{\mathbf{y}} + A_z\hat{\mathbf{z}}\), size \(|\mathbf{A}| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2 + A_z^2}\). Section 1.
  • Dot product. \(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B} = |\mathbf{A}||\mathbf{B}|\cos\theta = A_xB_x + A_yB_y + A_zB_z\), a number. Work done by a force \(\mathbf{F}\) (N) along a path: \(W = \int\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{l}\) (J). Section 2, section 7.
  • Cross product. \(|\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}| = |\mathbf{A}||\mathbf{B}|\sin\theta\), at right angles to both by the right-hand rule. Magnetic force \(\mathbf{F} = q\,\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\): \(q\) in C, \(\mathbf{v}\) in m/s, \(\mathbf{B}\) in T, \(\mathbf{F}\) in N. Section 3.
  • Circular motion. Acceleration \(v^2/R\) (m/s\(^2\)) towards the centre, for speed \(v\) on a circle of radius \(R\). Section 4.
  • Gradient. \(\nabla f = (\partial f/\partial x, \partial f/\partial y, \partial f/\partial z)\), pointing uphill; the field from a potential, \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}} = -\nabla V\) (V/m from V). Section 6.
  • Divergence and Gauss’s theorem. \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = \partial F_x/\partial x + \partial F_y/\partial y + \partial F_z/\partial z\); \(\displaystyle\oint_S\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{A} = \int_V\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}\,dV\): the flux out of a closed surface is the spreading inside it. Section 10.
  • Curl and Stokes’s theorem. \(\displaystyle\oint_C\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{A}\): the circulation round a loop is the swirl through any surface it bounds. Two identities: \(\nabla\times(\nabla f) = 0\) and \(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{F}) = 0\). Section 11.
  • Laplacian. \(\nabla^2f = \partial^2f/\partial x^2 + \partial^2f/\partial y^2 + \partial^2f/\partial z^2\), the divergence of the gradient. Section 12.

F3 Newtonian mechanics

  • Kinematics. \(\mathbf{v} = d\mathbf{r}/dt\), \(\mathbf{a} = d\mathbf{v}/dt\); at constant \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_0 + \mathbf{a}t\) and \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_0 + \mathbf{v}_0t + \tfrac12\mathbf{a}t^2\). Position in m, velocity in m/s, acceleration in m/s\(^2\). Section 1.
  • Newton’s second law. \(\mathbf{F} = d\mathbf{p}/dt = m\mathbf{a}\), momentum \(\mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}\) (kg m/s), force in N \(=\) kg m/s\(^2\). Impulse \(\Delta\mathbf{p} = \int\mathbf{F}\,dt\). Section 2, section 3.
  • Work, kinetic energy, power. \(\Delta W = \int\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{l}\), kinetic energy \(W = \tfrac12mv^2 = p^2/2m\) (J); power \(P = \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{v}\) (W). Section 4.
  • Potential energy. \(\mathbf{F} = -\nabla U\), and then \(W + U\) is constant; a charge at potential \(V\) has \(U = qV\), two charges \(U = q_1q_2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r)\) (J). Section 5.
  • Collisions. Centre-of-mass velocity \(\mathbf{V}_{cm} = (m_1\mathbf{v}_1 + m_2\mathbf{v}_2)/(m_1 + m_2)\); a body of kinetic energy \(W\) striking a mass \(m_2\) at rest makes \(W_{cm} = Wm_2/(m_1 + m_2)\) available; reduced mass \(\mu = m_1m_2/(m_1 + m_2)\). Section 6.
  • Rotation. \(\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}\) (kg m\(^2\)/s), torque \(\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}\) (N m), \(d\mathbf{L}/dt = \boldsymbol{\tau}\); a rigid body has \(L = I\omega\) and \(W = \tfrac12I\omega^2\), with \(I = \sum mr^2\) (kg m\(^2\)). Section 7.
  • Orbits. Under an attraction \(k/r^2\) (\(k\) in J m): circular speed \(v^2 = k/(mr)\), energy \(E = W + U = -k/2r\), negative for a bound orbit. Section 8.
  • Oscillator. \(m\ddot x = -kx\) gives \(x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)\), \(\omega = \sqrt{k/m}\), energy \(\tfrac12kA^2\); near a minimum of \(U(x)\), \(k = U''(x_0)\). Section 9.
  • Damped and driven. With damping the energy falls as \(e^{-\omega_0t/Q}\); driven at \(\omega_0\) the amplitude is \(Q\) times the static deflection, over a width \(\Delta\omega = \omega_0/Q\). Section 10.
  • Galilean change of frame. \(\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{V}t\), \(\mathbf{v}' = \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{V}\), \(\mathbf{a}' = \mathbf{a}\), for a frame moving at constant \(\mathbf{V}\). It fails near the speed of light. Section 11.

F4 Electricity and magnetism

  • Coulomb’s law. \(F = q_1q_2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2)\), charges in C, \(r\) in m, \(\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\) F/m. Section 2.
  • Lorentz force. \(\mathbf{F} = q(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}} + \mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\), \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\) in V/m, \(\mathbf{B}\) in T. Section 10.
  • Gauss’s law. \(\displaystyle\oint\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\cdot d\mathbf{A} = Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0\). Outside a charged sphere \(\mathcal{E} = Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2)\); a line of charge \(\lambda\) (C/m), \(\lambda/(2\pi\varepsilon_0r)\); a sheet of charge \(\sigma\) (C/m\(^2\)), \(\sigma/2\varepsilon_0\); between plates, \(\sigma/\varepsilon_0\). Section 3, section 4.
  • Potential. \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}} = -\nabla V\); energy gained \(W = q\,\Delta V\); between plates \(\mathcal{E} = V/d\); 1 eV \(= 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\) J. Section 5.
  • Capacitance. \(C = Q/V\) (F); plates \(C = \varepsilon_0A/d\), sphere \(C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0R\); stored energy \(\tfrac12CV^2\); energy density \(\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\mathcal{E}^2\) (J/m\(^3\)). Section 6.
  • Current and resistance. \(I = dQ/dt\) (A); \(I = nqv_dA\), with \(n\) carriers per m\(^3\) drifting at \(v_d\) through area \(A\); \(V = IR\), \(R = \rho L/A\) (Ω); power \(P = IV = I^2R\) (W). Section 8.
  • RC circuit. \(V = V_0e^{-t/RC}\), time constant \(\tau = RC\) (s). Section 9.
  • Magnetic fields of currents. Long straight wire \(B = \mu_0I/(2\pi r)\); loop of radius \(R\) on its axis \(B = \mu_0IR^2/[2(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}]\); long solenoid with \(n\) turns per metre \(B = \mu_0nI\); \(\mu_0 = 1.2566 \times 10^{-6}\) T m/A. Force on a wire \(\mathbf{F} = I\mathbf{L}\times\mathbf{B}\). Section 10, section 11, section 12.
  • Faraday’s law. EMF \(= -d\Phi_B/dt\), flux \(\Phi_B = \int\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{A}\) in Wb \(=\) T m\(^2\); a rod of length \(L\) moving at \(v\) across \(B\): EMF \(= BLv\). Section 14.
  • Inductance. \(V = L\,dI/dt\), \(L\) in henries (H); energy \(\tfrac12LI^2\); energy density \(B^2/2\mu_0\) (J/m\(^3\)); LR time constant \(L/R\). Section 15.
  • Maxwell’s equations. \(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}} = \rho/\varepsilon_0\), \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0\), \(\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}} = -\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}/\partial t\), with \(\rho\) in C/m\(^3\) and \(\mathbf{J}\) in A/m\(^2\). Section 16.
  • Light and energy flow. \(c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} = 2.998 \times 10^8\) m/s; in a wave \(\mathcal{E} = cB\); Poynting vector \(\mathbf{S} = \boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathbf{B}/\mu_0\) (W/m\(^2\)). Section 17, section 18.

F5 Oscillations and waves

  • Complex numbers. \(z = x + iy = re^{i\theta}\), \(i^2 = -1\), modulus \(r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\), argument \(\theta\); Euler’s formula \(e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta\). Section 1, section 2.
  • Oscillation as a turning arrow. \(x(t) = \mathrm{Re}(Xe^{i\omega t})\) with complex amplitude \(X = |X|e^{i\phi}\); \(d/dt\) becomes multiplication by \(i\omega\); \(\omega = 2\pi f\) (rad/s), \(f\) in Hz. Section 3.
  • Damped oscillator. \(\ddot x + 2\Gamma\dot x + \omega_0^2x = 0\) gives \(x = Ae^{-\Gamma t}\cos(\omega_dt + \phi)\), \(\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \Gamma^2}\), with damping rate \(\Gamma\) (s\(^{-1}\)). Section 4.
  • Quality factor. \(Q = \omega_0U/P = \omega_0/2\Gamma\), with \(U\) the stored energy (J) and \(P\) the power lost (W); the amplitude rings down with time constant \(2Q/\omega_0\). Section 4.
  • Driven oscillator. \(X = \dfrac{F_0/m}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 + 2i\Gamma\omega}\); near resonance the response is \(1/\sqrt{1 + y^2}\) of its peak, lagging by \(\arctan y\) more, with \(y = 2Q\,\Delta f/f_0\); full width at half power \(\Delta f = f_0/Q\). Section 5.
  • Impedances. \(Z_R = R\), \(Z_L = i\omega L\), \(Z_C = 1/(i\omega C)\), in Ω; \(V = ZI\) for complex amplitudes. \(LC\) resonance \(\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}\); series \(RLC\), \(Q = \omega_0L/R\). Section 6.
  • Beats. \(\cos\omega_1t + \cos\omega_2t = 2\cos\tfrac{(\omega_1 - \omega_2)t}{2}\cos\tfrac{(\omega_1 + \omega_2)t}{2}\); the loudness repeats at \(f_1 - f_2\). Section 7.
  • Wave equation. \(\partial^2y/\partial t^2 = v^2\,\partial^2y/\partial x^2\), solved by any \(f(x - vt) + g(x + vt)\); on a string \(v = \sqrt{T/\mu}\), tension \(T\) (N), mass per length \(\mu\) (kg/m). Section 8.
  • Sinusoidal wave. \(y = A\cos(\omega t - kx)\), wavenumber \(k = 2\pi/\lambda\) (rad/m), phase velocity \(v_{ph} = \omega/k = f\lambda\). Section 9.
  • Standing waves. Between fixed ends a distance \(L\) apart, \(f_n = nv/(2L)\), \(n = 1, 2, 3, \dots\) Section 10.
  • Reflection. On a line of impedance \(Z_0\) ending in \(Z_L\), the voltage reflection is \(\Gamma = (Z_L - Z_0)/(Z_L + Z_0)\), and the fraction \(\Gamma^2\) of the power is reflected. Section 11.
  • Dispersion. Group velocity \(v_g = d\omega/dk\); in a waveguide, \(\omega^2 = \omega_c^2 + c^2k^2\), so \(v_g = c\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2}\) and \(v_{ph}v_g = c^2\). Section 12.
  • Electromagnetic waves. \(c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} = 299\,792\,458\) m/s, \(B_0 = \mathcal{E}_0/c\), intensity \(I = \mathcal{E}_0^2/(2\eta_0)\) (W/m\(^2\)), \(\eta_0 = \sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0} = 376.7\) Ω. Section 13.
  • Doppler. For a slow source or observer approaching at speed \(u\), in a wave of speed \(v\): \(f' \approx f(1 + u/v)\). Section 14.
  • Interference and diffraction. Bright fringes at \(d\sin\theta = m\lambda\); nothing much smaller than \(\lambda\) can be resolved. Section 15.
  • Fourier. A periodic signal is a sum of sinusoids at multiples of its frequency; a pulse of length \(\Delta t\) (s) holds a band \(\Delta f \sim 1/\Delta t\) (Hz). Section 16.

F6 Heat, gases and probability

In sections 1 to 8 of F6, \(p\) is a probability and \(n\) a number of trials; from section 9 on, \(p\) is a pressure (Pa) and \(n\) a number density (m\(^{-3}\)).

  • Combining chances. Independent events: \(P(A\text{ and }B) = P(A)\,P(B)\). Exclusive events: \(P(A\text{ or }B) = P(A) + P(B)\). Section 1.
  • Binomial. \(k\) successes in \(n\) trials of probability \(p\): \(P(k) = \dbinom{n}{k}p^k(1 - p)^{n-k}\), mean \(np\), variance \(np(1 - p)\). Section 2.
  • Mean and spread. Mean \(\mu = \langle x\rangle\); variance \(\sigma^2 = \langle(x - \mu)^2\rangle = \langle x^2\rangle - \mu^2\); standard deviation \(\sigma\), in the units of \(x\). Section 3.
  • Gaussian. \(f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(x - \mu)^2/2\sigma^2}\); 68.3, 95.4 and 99.73 per cent within \(1\sigma\), \(2\sigma\), \(3\sigma\); full width at half maximum \(2.355\sigma\); from \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}dx = \sqrt\pi\). Section 5.
  • Poisson. \(P(N) = \mu^Ne^{-\mu}/N!\), mean \(\mu\), standard deviation \(\sqrt\mu\): a count of \(N\) is uncertain by about \(\sqrt N\). Section 6.
  • Errors. Independent errors add in quadrature, \(\sigma_{x+y} = \sqrt{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2}\); the mean of \(N\) readings has error \(\sigma/\sqrt N\). Section 8.
  • First law. \(\Delta U = Q - W_{by}\): internal energy \(U\), heat \(Q\) put in and work \(W_{by}\) done by the gas, all in J. Section 9.
  • Ideal gas. \(pV = Nk_BT = \nu RT\), or \(p = nk_BT\): \(p\) in Pa, \(V\) in m\(^3\), \(N\) molecules, \(\nu\) moles, \(n = N/V\), \(T\) in K, \(k_B = 1.380\,649 \times 10^{-23}\) J/K, \(R = N_Ak_B = 8.314\) J/(mol K). Section 10.
  • Kinetic theory. \(p = \tfrac13nm\langle v^2\rangle\), so \(\langle\tfrac12mv^2\rangle = \tfrac32k_BT\) and \(v_{rms} = \sqrt{3k_BT/m}\); each quadratic term in the energy holds \(\tfrac12k_BT\) on average. Section 11.
  • Boltzmann factor and mean speed. The probability of a state of energy \(E\) goes as \(e^{-E/k_BT}\); mean speed \(\bar v = \sqrt{8k_BT/\pi m}\) (m/s). Section 13.
  • Wall rate and mean free path. \(\Phi = \tfrac14n\bar v\) per m\(^2\) per second; \(\lambda = 1/(\sqrt2\,n\sigma)\) (m), with cross-section \(\sigma = \pi d^2\) for molecules of diameter \(d\). Section 14.
  • Second law and the price of cold. Entropy \(S\) (J/K) never falls in an isolated system; heat \(Q\) entering at \(T\) brings entropy \(Q/T\). The least work to pump heat \(Q\) from \(T\) up to room temperature \(T_a\) is \(W_{el} = Q(T_a - T)/T\). Section 16.

F7 Atoms, nuclei and quanta

  • Electron-volt and rest energies. 1 eV \(= 1.602\,176\,634 \times 10^{-19}\) J, the energy of a charge \(e\) across 1 V; \(m_ec^2 = 0.511\) MeV, \(m_pc^2 = 938.272\) MeV, 1 u \(= 931.494\) MeV\(/c^2\). Section 3.
  • Photon. \(E = hf = hc/\lambda\), momentum \(p = E/c = h/\lambda\), with \(hc = 1239.84\) eV nm \(= 1239.84\) MeV fm. Section 4.
  • X-ray tube. At voltage \(V\) the shortest wavelength is \(\lambda_{min} = hc/(eV)\). Section 5.
  • Bohr’s hydrogen. \(E_n = -13.606\ \text{eV}/n^2\), \(r_n = n^2a_0\) with \(a_0 = 0.0529\) nm; a photon from level \(n_2\) to \(n_1\) has \(hf = E_{n_2} - E_{n_1}\). Section 7.
  • Matter waves and uncertainty. De Broglie \(\lambda = h/p\); \(\Delta x\,\Delta p \gtrsim \hbar\), with \(\hbar = h/2\pi\) and \(\hbar c = 197.327\) MeV fm. Section 8.
  • Nuclear radius. \(R \approx 1.2\,A^{1/3}\) fm, with \(A\) the number of protons and neutrons. Section 10.
  • Binding energy and Q value. \(B = [Z\,m(^1\mathrm{H}) + Nm_n - M_{atom}]c^2\), with \(Z\) protons, \(N\) neutrons and atomic masses; \(Q = (\text{masses before} - \text{masses after})\,c^2\), positive when a reaction gives out energy. Section 11, section 12.
  • Decay. \(N = N_0e^{-\lambda_dt}\), half-life \(t_{1/2} = \ln2/\lambda_d\), activity \(\mathcal{A} = \lambda_dN\) in becquerels (decays per second); 1 Ci \(= 3.7 \times 10^{10}\) Bq. Section 13.
  • Pair creation. A photon needs at least \(2m_ec^2 = 1.022\) MeV to make an electron and a positron. Section 14.

2. Units and conversions

The course works in SI. Energies are in electron-volts with prefixes, momenta in eV/\(c\), masses as rest energies in eV. Older sources use other units; these are the conversions.

Energy, momentum and mass. - 1 eV \(= 1.602\,176\,634 \times 10^{-19}\) J exactly; 1 J \(= 6.241\,509 \times 10^{18}\) eV. - 1 GeV/\(c\) \(= 5.344\,286 \times 10^{-19}\) kg m s\(^{-1}\). - 1 eV of \(k_BT\) is 11 604.5 K. - A photon of energy \(\varepsilon\) has wavelength \(\lambda\,[\mathrm{nm}] = 1239.842/\varepsilon\,[\mathrm{eV}]\); 1 keV is 1.2398 nm.

Pressure. - 1 Pa \(=\) 1 N m\(^{-2}\); 1 mbar \(=\) 100 Pa; 1 bar \(= 10^5\) Pa. - 1 Torr \(=\) 1 mm of mercury \(= 101\,325/760\) Pa \(= 133.322\) Pa \(= 1.333\,22\) mbar. - 1 Pa \(= 7.500\,62 \times 10^{-3}\) Torr \(= 0.01\) mbar. - 1 atm \(=\) 101 325 Pa \(=\) 1013.25 mbar \(=\) 760 Torr. - Throughput: 1 mbar l s\(^{-1}\) \(=\) 0.1 Pa m\(^3\) s\(^{-1}\); 1 Torr l s\(^{-1}\) \(=\) 0.133 322 Pa m\(^3\) s\(^{-1}\). - Number density of an ideal gas at 20 °C: \(2.471 \times 10^{20}\) m\(^{-3}\) per pascal.

Magnetic field. 1 gauss (G) \(= 10^{-4}\) T; 1 T \(=\) 10 000 G; 1 kG \(=\) 0.1 T. (Lawrence’s “3,300 gauss” is 0.33 T.) The gauss belongs to the Gaussian system; the course never uses that system’s equations, only converts its numbers.

Area, cross-section and luminosity. - 1 barn (b) \(= 10^{-28}\) m\(^2\) \(= 10^{-24}\) cm\(^2\); 1 mb \(= 10^{-27}\) cm\(^2\); 1 nb \(= 10^{-33}\) cm\(^2\); 1 fb \(= 10^{-39}\) cm\(^2\). - 1 cm\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\) \(= 10^{4}\) m\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\), so \(10^{34}\) cm\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\) \(= 10^{38}\) m\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\). - Integrated luminosity: 1 fb\(^{-1}\) \(= 10^{39}\) cm\(^{-2}\) \(= 10^{43}\) m\(^{-2}\); 1 pb\(^{-1}\) \(= 10^{36}\) cm\(^{-2}\).

Current and particle number. A current of 1 µA carries \(6.2415 \times 10^{12}\) singly charged particles a second; in general \(I = \dot{N}q\).

Length. 1 inch \(=\) 25.4 mm exactly (cyclotrons were named by pole diameter in inches). 1 Å \(= 10^{-10}\) m \(=\) 0.1 nm.

Radiation. 1 Gy \(=\) 1 J kg\(^{-1}\) (absorbed dose); the sievert (Sv) is the same unit weighted for biological effect (chapter 8). 1 Bq \(=\) one decay a second.

Emittance. m rad, quoted as mm mrad or µm (the same unit), or nm and pm for electron rings. Check whether a source means rms or a multiple of rms (some use \(4\varepsilon\) or \(6\varepsilon\), and some put a factor \(\pi\) in front).

3. Practical formulas

Each comes with a check computed from the constants above. Singly charged particles unless stated.

Relativistic kinematics. \(\gamma = 1 + W/mc^2\), \(\beta = \sqrt{1 - 1/\gamma^2}\), \(pc = \sqrt{W(W + 2mc^2)}\), \(\beta\gamma = p/mc\). A proton of \(W = 1\) GeV has \(p = 1.696\) GeV/\(c\) and \(\beta = 0.875\).

Magnetic rigidity (chapter 1, section 4.1):

\[ B\rho = \frac{p}{q}, \qquad B\rho\,[\mathrm{T\,m}] = 3.3356\,p\,[\mathrm{GeV}/c] \quad (q = e) \]

For an ion of charge \(Ze\) divide by \(Z\). The 1 GeV proton above has \(B\rho = 5.657\) T m.

Cyclotron (gyration) frequency in a uniform field (chapter 1, section 4.2): \(f = qB/(2\pi\gamma m)\). For slow particles, 15.245 MHz per tesla for protons and 27.992 GHz per tesla for electrons.

Revolution frequency of a ring of circumference \(C\) (chapter 15, section 3): \(f_0 = \beta c/C\), \(\omega_0 = 2\pi f_0\), and the RF runs at \(f_{RF} = hf_0\). The 1 GeV proton in a 1000 m ring goes round at 262.3 kHz.

Synchrotron radiation, energy lost per turn in a ring whose bends all have radius \(\rho\) (chapter 20, section 3):

\[ U_0 = \frac{e^2\beta^3\gamma^4}{3\varepsilon_0\rho} = C_\gamma\,\frac{E^4}{\rho}, \qquad U_0\,[\mathrm{keV}] = 88.46\,\frac{E^4\,[\mathrm{GeV}]}{\rho\,[\mathrm{m}]} \quad \text{(electrons, } \beta \approx 1\text{)} \]

For protons the coefficient is \(7.78 \times 10^{-12}\) keV m GeV\(^{-4}\). The power the RF must give a beam of current \(I\) is \(P = U_0I/e\), so \(P\,[\mathrm{kW}] = U_0\,[\mathrm{keV}] \times I\,[\mathrm{A}]\). A 1 GeV electron on a 1 m radius loses 88.46 keV a turn.

Critical photon energy (chapter 20, section 5):

\[ \varepsilon_c = \frac{3\hbar c\gamma^3}{2\rho}, \qquad \varepsilon_c\,[\mathrm{keV}] = 2.218\,\frac{E^3\,[\mathrm{GeV}]}{\rho\,[\mathrm{m}]} = 0.665\,E^2\,[\mathrm{GeV}]\,B\,[\mathrm{T}], \qquad \lambda_c\,[\mathrm{nm}] = 0.559\,\frac{\rho\,[\mathrm{m}]}{E^3\,[\mathrm{GeV}]} \]

The 1 GeV electron on 1 m: \(\varepsilon_c = 2.218\) keV.

Undulator strength and wavelength (chapter 20, section 11):

\[ K = \frac{eB_0\lambda_u}{2\pi m_ec} = 0.934\,B_0\,[\mathrm{T}]\,\lambda_u\,[\mathrm{cm}], \qquad \lambda_n = \frac{\lambda_u}{2n\gamma^2}\left(1 + \frac{K^2}{2} + \gamma^2\theta^2\right) \]

On axis, in practical units, \(\lambda_1\,[\mathrm{nm}] = 1.306\,\lambda_u\,[\mathrm{cm}]\,(1 + K^2/2)/E^2\,[\mathrm{GeV}]\), or \(\varepsilon_1\,[\mathrm{keV}] = 0.950\,E^2\,[\mathrm{GeV}]/(\lambda_u\,[\mathrm{cm}]\,(1 + K^2/2))\). A 1 T peak field with a 2 cm period gives \(K = 1.87\); at 3 GeV with \(\lambda_u = 2\) cm and \(K = 1\) the fundamental is 0.435 nm, 2.85 keV.

Electron cyclotron resonance (chapter 7, ECR sources): \(B_{ECR} = 2\pi f_{RF}m_e/e\), that is 35.72 mT per GHz, or \(f\,[\mathrm{GHz}] = 27.99\,B\,[\mathrm{T}]\). 87.5 mT at 2.45 GHz; 0.518 T at 14.5 GHz.

Plasma frequency, Debye length, wave-breaking field (chapter 7, plasmas; chapter 27, section 1):

\[ \omega_p = \sqrt{\frac{ne^2}{\varepsilon_0 m_e}}, \qquad \lambda_D = \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0 k_BT_e}{ne^2}}, \qquad \mathcal{E}_0 = \frac{m_ec\,\omega_p}{e} \]

In practical units \(f_p\,[\mathrm{Hz}] = 8.979\sqrt{n\,[\mathrm{m^{-3}}]}\), \(\lambda_D\,[\mathrm{m}] = 7434\sqrt{k_BT_e\,[\mathrm{eV}]/n\,[\mathrm{m^{-3}}]}\), and \(\mathcal{E}_0\,[\mathrm{V/m}] = 96.16\sqrt{n\,[\mathrm{cm^{-3}}]}\). At \(n = 10^{18}\) cm\(^{-3}\), \(\mathcal{E}_0 = 96\) GV/m and \(f_p = 9.0\) THz.

Laser strength parameter (chapter 27, section 2): \(a_0 = eE_L/(m_ec\,\omega_0) = 0.855\,\lambda_0\,[\mu\mathrm{m}]\sqrt{I/10^{18}\ \mathrm{W\,cm^{-2}}}\) for linear polarisation.

Spin tune of electrons (chapter 18, section 13): \(\nu = a_e\gamma = E/440.65\) MeV.

Polarisation time, Sokolov-Ternov (chapter 20, section 12): \(\tau_p\,[\mathrm{s}] = 98.7\,\rho^3\,[\mathrm{m}]/E^5\,[\mathrm{GeV}]\) for an isomagnetic ring, times \(R/\rho\) with straight sections.

Kilpatrick criterion (chapter 13, section 10): \(f\,[\mathrm{MHz}] = 1.64\,\mathcal{E}_K^2\,e^{-8.5/\mathcal{E}_K}\) with \(\mathcal{E}_K\) in MV/m; 18.5 MV/m at 352.2 MHz.

Muon turns in one lifetime (chapter 27, section 10): \(N = \tau_\mu e\bar{B}/(2\pi m_\mu) = 297\,\bar{B}\,[\mathrm{T}]\), whatever the energy.

Part I. The toolkit

Chapter 1. Why accelerate, and the toolkit

  • Energy and momentum. \(L = -mc^2/\gamma\), \(\mathbf{p} = \gamma m\mathbf{v}\), \(E = \gamma mc^2\), \(E^2 = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2\), \(W = (\gamma - 1)mc^2\). Lagrangian (J), momentum (kg m/s), energies (J). The relativistic particle from its action. Section 2.1.
  • Relations. \(\gamma = E/mc^2 = 1 + W/mc^2\); \(\beta = pc/E\); \(\beta\gamma = p/mc\); \(pc = \sqrt{W(W + 2mc^2)}\). Section 2.2.
  • Momentum spread from energy spread. \(\dfrac{dp}{p} = \dfrac{1}{\beta^2}\dfrac{dW}{E} = \dfrac{\gamma}{\gamma + 1}\dfrac{dW}{W}\). Rings sort by momentum; a 1 per cent energy spread is 0.5 per cent in momentum for slow particles, 1 per cent for fast ones. Section 2.2.
  • Lorentz force and energy. \(d\mathbf{p}/dt = q(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}} + \mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\) (N); \(dE/dt = q\,\mathbf{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\) (W). Only the electric field changes the energy. Section 3.
  • Static potential. \(\Delta W = q(\phi_1 - \phi_2) = qV\) (J; \(V\) in volts), whatever the path. Section 3.1.
  • Rigidity and gyration. \(\rho = p_\perp/(qB)\); \(B\rho = p/q\) (T m); \(\omega = qB/(\gamma m)\) (rad/s). Sections 4.1 and 4.2.
  • De Broglie wavelength. \(\lambda = h/p\) (m); \(\lambda\,[\mathrm{fm}] = 1239.842/pc\,[\mathrm{MeV}]\). A probe resolves down to about its wavelength. Section 1.1.
  • Coulomb barrier. \(V_C = zZe^2/[4\pi\varepsilon_0(R_1 + R_2)]\), \(R_1 + R_2 = r_0(A_1^{1/3} + A_2^{1/3})\), with \(r_0\) about 1.2 fm (charge radii) to 1.4 to 1.5 fm (barriers). The classical energy needed to touch a nucleus. Section 1.2.
  • Invariant mass. \(s = (\sum E_i)^2 - |\sum\mathbf{p}_ic|^2\) (eV\(^2\)). Fixed target: \(s = m_1^2c^4 + m_2^2c^4 + 2Em_2c^2\), so \(\sqrt{s} \approx \sqrt{2Em_2c^2}\) for \(E \gg mc^2\); head-on equal beams: \(\sqrt{s} = 2E\). Section 5.
  • Antiproton threshold. \(p + p \to p + p + p + \bar{p}\) needs \(E = 7m_pc^2\), \(W = 6m_pc^2 = 5.63\) GeV. Section 1.3.
  • Rate on a target. \(R = \dot{N}nt\sigma\) (s\(^{-1}\)), valid while \(nt\sigma \ll 1\); \(\mathcal{L} = \dot{N}nt\) is the fixed-target luminosity. Section 6.1.
  • Rutherford cross-section. \(\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \left(\dfrac{zZe^2}{16\pi\varepsilon_0W}\right)^2\dfrac{1}{\sin^4(\theta/2)}\) (m\(^2\) sr\(^{-1}\)), point charges, no recoil. Section 6.2.

Chapter 2. The cathode ray tube

  • Speed after a potential. \(W = (\gamma - 1)mc^2 = |q|V\); for \(|q|V \ll mc^2\), \(v = \sqrt{2|q|V/m}\). Section 1.
  • Electric deflection on a screen. \(Y = \dfrac{l\,L_s\,V_d}{2\,d\,V_a}\) (m): plates of length \(l\) and gap \(d\), deflecting voltage \(V_d\), beam voltage \(V_a\), screen \(L_s\) from the plates’ centre. Sensitivity \(Y/V_d\) in m/V. Section 2.
  • Magnetic bending. \(r = p/(|q|B)\); over a flight path \(L \ll r\) the spot moves \(y \approx L^2/(2r)\). Section 3.
  • Thomson’s crossed fields. \(v = \mathcal{E}/B\), \(e/m = \mathcal{E}\theta_E/(B^2l)\), with \(\mathcal{E}\) the electric field (V/m). Opposed fields pass only speed \(\mathcal{E}/B\): a velocity filter. Section 4.
  • Richardson-Dushman law. \(J = A_RT^2\exp(-e\phi_w/k_BT)\) (A/m\(^2\)), with \(\phi_w\) the work function in volts in this chapter and \(A_R = 4\pi m_eek_B^2/h^3 = 1.202 \times 10^6\) A m\(^{-2}\) K\(^{-2}\) in theory (Dushman’s tungsten fit: 60.2 A cm\(^{-2}\) K\(^{-2}\), 4.53 V). Section 6.
  • Child-Langmuir law. \(J = \dfrac49\varepsilon_0\sqrt{\dfrac{2e}{m_e}}\,\dfrac{V_a^{3/2}}{d^2} = 2.334 \times 10^{-6}\,\dfrac{V_a^{3/2}}{d^2}\) A/m\(^2\) (\(V_a\) in V, \(d\) in m). The potential goes as \(V_a(x/d)^{4/3}\). The space-charge limit of a planar diode. Section 7.
  • Knee of a diode. \(V_{knee} = (J_{RD}d^2/2.334 \times 10^{-6})^{2/3}\): below it space charge limits the current, above it the cathode temperature. Section 7.
  • Busch’s theorem and the solenoid lens. \(\dot\theta = -qB_z/(2\gamma m)\); \(\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{4(B\rho)^2}\displaystyle\int B_z^2\,dz \to \dfrac{e}{8m_eV_a}\displaystyle\int B_z^2\,dz\) (non-relativistic). For one thin loop of radius \(a\) and \(NI\) ampere-turns, \(f = 97.8\,V_aa/(NI)^2\) m. Section 9.
  • Screen brightness. \(L = K_L(V_a - V_0)^n\), \(n\) about 1.4 to 3, \(V_0\) the dead voltage. Section 10.
  • Electron mean free path in gas. \(\lambda_e = 4k_BT/(\pi d_m^2p) = 4\sqrt2\,\lambda\), with \(p\) the pressure (Pa) and \(d_m\) the molecular diameter; the fraction crossing \(L\) unscattered is \(e^{-L/\lambda_e}\). Section 11.

Chapter 3. Vacuum

In this chapter \(P\) is the pressure (Pa) and \(Q\) the gas load or throughput (Pa m\(^3\)/s).

  • Ideal gas. \(P = nk_BT\), \(n\) in m\(^{-3}\). Pressure, units and number density.
  • Mean molecular speed. \(\bar v = \sqrt{8k_BT/(\pi m)}\) (m/s); 471 m/s for nitrogen at 20 °C. The speeds of molecules.
  • Wall flux. \(\Phi = \frac14 n\bar v = P/\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}\) (m\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\)). How often molecules hit a wall.
  • Monolayer time. \(t_{\mathrm{mono}} = N_s/\Phi\) with \(N_s \approx 10^{19}\) m\(^{-2}\): about an hour at \(10^{-7}\) Pa. The monolayer time.
  • Mean free path. \(\lambda = k_BT/(\sqrt2\,\pi d^2P)\); for a fast electron \(\lambda_e = 4\sqrt2\,\lambda\), a lower bound on what crosses. Molecule, fast electron.
  • Knudsen number. \(Kn = \lambda/D\): viscous flow below 0.01, molecular above 0.5. Flow regimes.
  • Conductance. \(C = Q/(P_1 - P_2)\) (m\(^3\)/s); orifice \(C = \frac14\bar vA\) (11.6 l/s per cm\(^2\) for air); long tube \(C = \frac{\pi}{12}\bar v D^3/L\) (12.1 \(D^3/L\) l/s for air, cm); in series \(1/C = 1/C_1 + 1/C_2\). Conductance in molecular flow.
  • Effective pumping speed. \(1/S_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1/S + 1/C\). Pumping speed and the effective speed.
  • Pump-down. \(V\,dP/dt = Q - SP\); \(P(t) = Q/S + (P_0 - Q/S)e^{-t/\tau}\), \(\tau = V/S\); ultimate pressure \(P_{\mathrm{ult}} = Q/S\). The pump-down equation.
  • Outgassing of unbaked metal. \(q_A \approx 3 \times 10^{-9}/t\,[\mathrm{h}]\) mbar l s\(^{-1}\) cm\(^{-2}\); the \(1/t\) comes from a spread of binding energies with sojourn time \(\tau = \tau_0e^{E_d/k_BT}\). Outgassing.
  • Gauges. McLeod: \(P = \rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}gah^2/V\). Ionisation gauge: \(I_c = S_gI_eP\), \(S_g \approx 10\) Torr\(^{-1}\) for nitrogen. How gauges work.
  • Beam-gas lifetime. \(1/\tau_{\mathrm{beam}} = v\sum_in_i\sigma_i\). Vacuum for beams.
  • Ion-induced pressure runaway. \(P = P_0/[1 - \eta_i\sigma_{\mathrm{ion}}I/(eS')]\), diverging at \((\eta_iI)_{\mathrm{crit}} = eS'/\sigma_{\mathrm{ion}}\), with \(S'\) the pumping speed per metre (m\(^2\)/s). Vacuum for beams.

Part VI. Light, new methods and uses

Chapter 26. Free-electron lasers and energy recovery

In this chapter \(\rho_F\) is the Pierce parameter and \(\delta\) the relative energy deviation.

  • Coherence. \(P(k) = N^2|b(k)|^2P_1(k)\), \(b(k) = \langle e^{-ikz}\rangle\). Section 1.
  • Resonance. \(\lambda_r = \dfrac{\lambda_u}{2\gamma^2}\left(1 + \dfrac{K^2}{2}\right)\). Section 2.
  • Pendulum equations. \(\theta = (k_r + k_u)s - \omega_rt + \phi_0\); \(d\theta/ds = 2k_u\delta\), \(d\delta/ds = -\dfrac{eK[JJ]\mathcal{E}_L}{2\gamma_r^2mc^2}\sin\theta\), \([JJ] = J_0(\xi) - J_1(\xi)\), \(\xi = K^2/(4 + 2K^2)\). Section 3, section 4.
  • Low gain (Madey, quoted). \(G(\nu) = (4\pi N_u\rho_F)^3(-d/d\nu)[\sin(\nu/2)/(\nu/2)]^2\), \(\nu = 4\pi N_u\delta_0\). Section 5.
  • High gain. \(d^3b/ds^3 = i(2k_u\rho_F)^3b\); \(\rho_F = \left[\dfrac1{16}\dfrac{I}{I_A}\dfrac{K^2[JJ]^2}{\gamma_r^3\sigma_x^2k_u^2}\right]^{1/3}\); power gain length \(L_G = \lambda_u/(4\pi\sqrt3\rho_F)\). Section 6.
  • Saturation. \(P_{sat} \approx \rho_FP_b\), \(P_b = (E/e)I\), after about 18 to 20 gain lengths. Section 7.
  • Beam requirements. \(\sigma_\delta \lesssim \rho_F\); \(\varepsilon \lesssim \lambda_r/(4\pi)\), \(\varepsilon_n \lesssim \gamma\lambda_r/(4\pi)\). Section 8.
  • Chicane (this chapter’s sign: \(R_{56} < 0\) for a chicane). \(z_f = z_i - R_{56}\delta\); \(R_{56} \approx -2\theta_B^2(\Delta L + \frac23L_B)\); compression \(C = 1/|1 - R_{56}\chi|\); chirp from off-crest RF \(\chi = -e\hat Vk_{RF}\cos\phi_s/E_f\). Section 9.
  • CSR and the laser heater. \(\langle dW/ds\rangle \approx -0.35\,Nr_emc^2/(\rho^{2/3}\sigma_z^{4/3})\); ripple suppression \(e^{-(kR_{56}\sigma_\delta)^2/2}\). Section 10.
  • Harmonic bunching (HGHG). \(|b_{n_h}| = |J_{n_h}(n_h\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D})|e^{-n_h^2\mathcal{D}^2/2}\). Section 11.
  • Generator power. \(P_g = \dfrac{\hat V^2}{4(R/Q)Q_L}\left[\left(1 + \dfrac{(R/Q)Q_LI_{net}}{\hat V}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{2Q_L\Delta f}{f_{RF}}\right)^2\right]\); with energy recovery, \(P_g = \hat V^2\Delta f/[(R/Q)f_{RF}]\). Section 12.
  • Transverse deflector resolution. \(\sigma_{z,res} = \dfrac{E}{eV_\perp k_{RF}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\beta_{y,d}}}\dfrac{1}{|\sin\Delta\psi|}\). At the bench.
